Mentor guide · London · 13 October 2026

Ask,
don’t drive.

Teams have under two hours. Don’t fix their code. Ask the question that helps them cut scope, name a real user and protect the demo.

1 One question at each stage

  1. 18:30
    Form teamsWho is it for, and what one thing will you show?Pair up solo builders.
  2. 18:45
    Start buildingCould anyone build this in a weekend?Push for something new early.
  3. 19:00
    Build checkWhat works end to end right now?Find the one blocker. Mock the rest.
  4. 20:00
    Finish and recordWhat does the 1:30 video need?No new features. Keep a backup take.
  5. 20:20
    Ten-minute warningIs your entry in?Entries close at 20:30 sharp.

2 Where teams get stuck

Too many features→One person, one problem, one flow.
No clear user→Name a real person and their money problem.
Feels generic→Ask what is new. Reuse Open Banking or credit data.
AI code breaks→Rerun the main flow on fresh data. Check logins and secrets.
Demo fails→Mock slow calls. Keep a backup recording.
Quiet or new→Agree roles early. Praise small wins.

3 Do and don’t

Do

  • Ask, listen, let them choose
  • Say when you’ll be back
  • Help pick roles and a narrator
  • Name a strength before a fix

Don’t

  • Take the keyboard
  • Pitch your own idea
  • Push a pivot after 19:30
  • Solve it for them

4 The 1:30 demo

  1. 0:00“Meet [name]. [Their money problem].”
  2. 0:15“Until now [this was hard]. Now [what changed].”
  3. 0:25One flow, live. The wow moment by 1:00.
  4. 1:05“Real: [this]. Mocked: [that].”
  5. 1:20Who pays, and why. Stop at 1:30.
It feels better to have something small working at the end than something big that’s in shambles.

Jarek Wojciechowski, Atomic Object, on mentoring hackathon teams
